## Why Hard Money Closes So Much Faster Than a Bank

The speed difference between hard money and a conventional loan isn't a marketing trick — it's structural. A bank underwrites the *borrower* first: it verifies income with tax returns and W-2s, runs debt-to-income ratios, orders employment verifications, and pushes the file through layers of committee approval. That documentation-heavy process is what eats 30 to 45 days.

A hard money lender underwrites the *asset* first. The loan is secured by the property, so the central question is whether the deal makes sense — the purchase price, the [after-repair value](/blog/how-to-calculate-arv), and the exit. Because private lenders like Funded Capital require **no income verification on most programs**, the biggest time sink in traditional lending simply disappears.

That shift changes everything about the timeline. Instead of waiting on payroll departments and underwriting committees, the clock is driven by a short list of concrete tasks: valuing the property, clearing title, and signing documents. Understanding those tasks is how you understand the timeline — and how you shorten it. ## The Hard Money Closing Timeline, Stage by Stage

Here's what actually happens between "I found a deal" and "the wire hit." Each stage has a typical duration, but they overlap more than most investors expect — a good lender runs several in parallel.

| Stage | What happens | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| Application & term sheet | You submit deal details; lender issues terms | Same day — 24 hours |
| Underwriting & valuation | Lender reviews the deal; appraisal or BPO ordered | 1–5 days |
| Title & insurance | Title search, clearing liens, hazard insurance bound | 2–7 days |
| Document prep & signing | Loan docs drawn, reviewed, signed at closing | 1–3 days |
| Funding | Lender wires funds; deal records | Same day as signing |

### Stage 1: Application and Term Sheet (Hours)

The process starts when you submit the deal — property address, purchase price, rehab budget if it's a [fix and flip](/fix-and-flip-loans), and your exit plan. A responsive private lender turns this around fast. At Funded Capital, we issue a **term sheet in two hours**, so you know your rate, leverage, and terms almost immediately and can make a credible offer the same day.

### Stage 2: Underwriting and Valuation (1–5 Days)

Once you accept the term sheet, the lender validates the deal. This is where a property valuation — a full appraisal or, on many programs, a faster broker price opinion — gets ordered. Valuation is frequently the single longest step in the whole timeline, because it depends on a third-party appraiser's schedule, not the lender's. Underwriting the borrower's credit and reserves happens in parallel and rarely holds things up.

### Stage 3: Title and Insurance (2–7 Days)

While valuation runs, the title company performs a title search to confirm clear ownership and surface any liens, judgments, or clouds that have to be cleared before closing. You'll also need to bind a hazard insurance policy naming the lender. Title is another step controlled by an outside party, and on a property with a messy history it can become the bottleneck.

### Stage 4: Document Preparation and Signing (1–3 Days)

With valuation and title in hand, the lender draws the loan documents. You review them, then sign — often at a title company or with a mobile notary. For borrowers who close as an [LLC](/blog/llc-real-estate-loan), the entity paperwork is confirmed here, which is why having your LLC formed in advance matters.

### Stage 5: Funding (Same Day)

After signing, the lender wires the funds and the deal records. On a purchase, the seller gets paid and the property is yours. On a [refinance](/blog/refinance-hard-money-loan-to-dscr), proceeds pay off the existing loan and any cash-out reaches you. This step is fast — the work was all in the stages before it.

## What Speeds Up — and Slows Down — Your Closing

The five-day close is real, but it's earned. The single biggest variable in the entire timeline is the borrower. Deals close fast when the borrower moves fast, and they stall when documents trickle in one at a time.

### What Accelerates the Timeline

The fastest closings share a few traits. The borrower has their entity documents, ID, bank statements, and insurance ready before the term sheet is even signed. The property has clean title and easy comparable sales. The purchase contract is realistic and the numbers on the deal hold up under a valuation. And the lender is a direct, decision-making private lender — not a broker passing your file to a capital source you never speak to. The difference between [private money and hard money](/blog/private-money-vs-hard-money) and a middleman shop often shows up right here, in days saved.

### What Slows It Down

Delays almost always come from the same handful of places: a slow appraisal in a hot market where every appraiser is booked, title problems like unresolved liens or probate issues, a borrower who's slow to return signed documents or provide requested items, or a property that appraises below the number the deal was built on. Insurance can also stall a closing if it's left to the last minute. Notice that most of these live outside the lender's four walls — which is exactly why a lender who manages these relationships tightly closes faster.

### How to Prepare for the Fastest Possible Close

If you want to hit the five-day timeline, get ahead of the third parties. Have your [LLC](/blog/llc-real-estate-loan) formed and your operating agreement handy. Line up an insurance agent who can bind a policy same-day. Keep a clean, current set of financials — bank statements and a schedule of your other properties — in a folder you can send in one email. And know your numbers cold, because a deal that survives underwriting on the first pass is a deal that closes on schedule. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to close a hard money loan?**

A hard money loan typically closes in one to two weeks, and can close in as little as five days when the deal and borrower are well prepared. That's dramatically faster than a conventional mortgage, which usually takes 30 to 45 days, because hard money lenders underwrite the property rather than verifying the borrower's income. At Funded Capital, we issue term sheets in two hours and close in as little as five days.

**What's the fastest a hard money loan can close?**

The fastest closings happen in about five business days. Hitting that timeline requires a clean-title property, an easy valuation, and a borrower who has their entity documents, insurance, and financials ready to go from day one. The main constraints are third-party steps like the appraisal and title search, so the more of your side you prepare in advance, the closer you get to the five-day floor.

**Why is hard money faster than a bank loan?**

Banks underwrite the borrower first — verifying income with tax returns and W-2s, running debt-to-income ratios, and passing files through committees — which takes weeks. Hard money lenders underwrite the asset, focusing on the property's value and the deal's exit. With no income verification required on most programs, the biggest time sink in traditional lending is removed, leaving only valuation, title, and signing to drive the timeline.

**What slows down a hard money closing?**

The most common delays come from a slow appraisal in a busy market, title problems like unresolved liens or probate issues, insurance left to the last minute, or a borrower who's slow to return documents. A property that appraises below the deal's assumed value can also force a re-trade. Most of these live outside the lender's control, which is why working with a lender who manages appraiser and title relationships tightly makes a real difference.
