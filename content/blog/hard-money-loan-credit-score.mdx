Every private lender's website hedges on credit. "Flexible guidelines." "Credit considered, not decisive." Nobody prints a number, so investors apply blind and find out at term-sheet time.

Here's ours, in plain terms: most Funded Capital programs look for a **660+ mid-FICO**, our strongest leverage tiers open at **680**, and **640–659 is case-by-case** — a strong deal can carry an average score. Below 640, we're usually not the right lender until the score recovers.

But the number is the least interesting part. On an asset-based loan, your credit score doesn't decide whether you get a yes. It decides how much cash you bring to closing. This post covers what a hard money lender actually pulls, the four factors that outrank your score, and exactly what to do if you're sitting at 640.

## What a Hard Money Lender Pulls — and When

A hard money credit check is not a mortgage credit check. At [Funded Capital](/apply), the sequence looks like this:

**At application:** a soft pull. It doesn't touch your score. We're reading your mid-score (the middle of your three bureau scores) to place you in a leverage tier for the term sheet — which is how we can turn one around in 2 hours.

**In underwriting:** a formal credit report. Here we're not studying your card utilization like a consumer lender. We're scanning for a short list of disqualifiers: recent mortgage lates, an active foreclosure or bankruptcy, and open judgments or tax liens that could jump ahead of our lien position. A 12-year-old charge-off from a store card is noise. An unresolved tax lien is not — it's a title problem wearing a credit costume.

That's the whole exam. No tax returns, no W-2s, no debt-to-income calculation. The property's numbers do the heavy lifting — that's what "asset-based" means in practice.

## The Tiers: What 640, 680, and 740 Actually Get You

Credit gates leverage before it gates approval. Here's how score bands typically translate on a fix and flip loan in 2026:

| Mid-FICO | What It Means | Typical Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 740+ | Top tier | Maximum leverage tier, best pricing in range |
| 680–739 | Standard tier | Full program access, strong leverage |
| 660–679 | Entry tier | Approved with modestly reduced leverage |
| 640–659 | Case-by-case | Deal strength must carry the file; more cash in |
| Below 640 | Usually out | Rebuild first — see the plan below |

Notice what's *not* on this table: a band where the answer flips from yes to no based on score alone between 660 and 850. That's the practical difference between a hard money lender and a bank. A bank underwrites you. We underwrite the deal, then use your score to calibrate how much of it we'll finance.

Rates work the same way. Fix and flip pricing at Funded Capital starts at 8.75%, and where you land in the range depends on leverage, experience, and the deal — with credit as one input, not the master switch. ## The Four Factors That Outrank Your Credit Score

If credit only sets the tier, what decides the yes? Four things, in roughly this order.

### 1. Equity Position

How far below value are you buying, and how much of your own cash is in the deal? A purchase at 65% of after-repair value with a real down payment gives the lender a cushion no credit score can replicate. This is the single strongest line on your application. Buying right — at or below your [70%-rule maximum](/blog/70-percent-rule-house-flipping) — matters more than 40 FICO points.

### 2. Exit Strategy

Every short-term loan is underwritten against its exit. A flip needs supportable [ARV comps](/blog/how-to-calculate-arv) and a realistic days-on-market assumption. A BRRRR needs rents that clear a refinance — ideally into a [30-year DSCR loan](/dscr-loans). A vague exit is a bigger red flag to an underwriter than a 655 score.

### 3. Experience

Completed projects are priced. Ten flips is a different risk than a first deal, and leverage tiers reflect it. First-timers still get funded — we wrote a whole guide on [hard money loans for first-time flippers](/blog/hard-money-loans-first-time-flippers) — but experience can buy back leverage that credit alone won't.

### 4. Liquidity

Six to twelve months of visible reserves — cash or securities, not equity trapped in other properties — tells the lender you can carry the project when the timeline slips. And timelines slip: that's precisely the scenario lenders price against.

Get those four right and an average score becomes a footnote. Get them wrong and an 800 won't save the file.

## Two Borrowers, Same Deal: What the Score Actually Costs

Illustrative example — same $280K Tampa duplex, same $70K rehab budget, same $420K ARV:

**Borrower A** — 712 mid-FICO, three completed flips. Qualifies at 88% loan-to-cost: roughly **$33K cash in** at closing.

**Borrower B** — 664 mid-FICO, one completed flip. Qualifies at 78% loan-to-cost: roughly **$75K cash in** at closing.

Same deal. Same lender. Both approved. The $42K difference is the real price of the credit gap — it showed up as required equity, not as a rejection letter. That's why the question to ask any hard money lender is not "what's your minimum score?" but "show me your leverage tier sheet."

## At 640? Here's the 90-Day Rebuild

If your mid-score is sitting in the low 600s, you're one or two reporting cycles from lendable. In rough order of impact:

1. **Pay revolving balances below 30% utilization** — below 10% is better. Utilization has no memory; the score responds the month the balances report.
2. **Clear small collections and any open judgments or tax liens.** The liens matter double: they're both a score drag and a title problem.
3. **Don't open new accounts** and don't close old ones — age of credit is working for you.
4. **Pull all three reports and dispute errors.** Mid-score means the middle number; fixing one bureau can move your lendable score even if the other two don't budge.
5. **Keep every mortgage and rent payment spotless** — recent housing lates are the one item asset-based lenders genuinely can't look past.

While the score recovers, build the rest of the file: document reserves, tighten your rehab budget, line up comps.
