Your bridge loan has a maturity date. Your tenants don't know it exists. Stabilizing a value-add apartment building on time comes down to the gap between those two facts.

Three numbers frame the market this fall. According to Apartment List's August 2026 data, published in late September 2026, national apartment vacancy was 7.08% and median time on market was 31.7 days. In its September 2026 national rent report, Apartment List said units leased that month had been listed for an average of 34 days. Realtor.com data reported by CRE Daily on September 20, 2026 showed concessions on 43.5% of listings across the 50 largest metros in August.

So it takes longer to lease units, and many of them only lease with a discount. Here's how to plan for that.

## The clock starts at the refinance, not at closing

Most value-add plans end with a permanent loan, and that loan has an occupancy test. Fannie Mae's Conventional Properties term sheet (2026) requires stabilized occupancy, "typically 90%", for 90 days before funding. It considers pre-stabilized properties case by case.

Small buildings face a tougher test. Fannie Mae's Multifamily Selling and Servicing Guide, effective September 28, 2026, sets the small-loan rule by size. A property with 10 or more units needs at least 90% physical occupancy for the 90 days before the commitment date. A property with fewer than 10 units can have no more than one vacant unit at commitment, and it needs at least 90% average occupancy over the prior 12 months.

If you own a 6-unit or 8-unit building, that 12-month average matters most. A messy lease-up year stays in the numbers even after the building is full.

Agency debt is one exit. A DSCR loan or a bank loan is another, and each lender sets its own occupancy and seasoning rules. Get the takeout lender's test in writing before you close the bridge.

## Work backward from maturity

Four blocks of time sit between closing and payoff:

1. **Renovate** the vacant and down units.
2. **Lease up** to the occupancy target.
3. **Season**: the months the takeout lender wants to see at that occupancy.
4. **Refinance**: appraisal, underwriting, title, closing.

Here's how that adds up for a 24-unit building bought with 14 units occupied. At 90%, the target is 22 occupied units (21.6, rounded up). That means 8 net new leases.

| Block | Months | What drives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renovate 10 vacant units in batches | 4 | Contractor capacity, draw timing |
| Lease up from 14 to 22 occupied | 4 | 2 net leases per month |
| Season at 90% or better | 3 | Takeout lender's 90-day test |
| Refinance process | 2 | Appraisal, underwriting, title |
| **Total** | **13** | |

*Illustrative. Your numbers come from your contractor, your property manager and your takeout lender.*

Requity Group's 2026 multifamily bridge guide puts typical bridge terms at 12 to 24 months. It says lenders often offer extensions six months at a time, for a fee. On a 12-month loan, a 13-month plan needs an extension before you've signed a single lease. On an 18-month loan, the same plan has 5 months of cushion.

You can shorten the plan by overlapping blocks. If each batch of renovated units goes on the market the week it's finished, renovation and lease-up run at the same time. That's the biggest time saving available to you.

## Lease-up pace: the number that breaks most plans

Most pro formas get this number wrong, and it's usually too optimistic.

Two traps:

- **Net, not gross.** Existing tenants move out while you lease. If you sign 3 leases in a month and 1 tenant leaves, you gained 2. Budget for normal turnover on the occupied units.
- **Seasonality.** Apartment List's September 2026 report notes that time on market is seasonal and rises in the slow months. If renovations finish in November, you'll be leasing through winter. Plan for slower leasing that quarter.

Track the leasing funnel every week: leads, tours, applications, approvals, move-ins. When one stage stalls, you'll see it in days, not at month-end.

Local data beats national data. Apartment List's August 2026 figures had San Francisco at 20.7 days on market against 31.7 nationally. Pull comps from your own submarket and building class. ## Vacancy and concessions: underwrite what tenants actually pay

The sources don't agree on vacancy, so don't rely on just one. Apartments.com's September 2026 rent report, published September 28, 2026, put national vacancy at 7.8%. Census data reported by The Real Deal on February 3, 2026 put fourth-quarter 2025 rental vacancy at 7.2%, with the South highest at 9.1%. Use the number for your submarket and your class of building.

Concessions are the hidden cost. The Realtor.com figures reported by CRE Daily showed concessions rising from 40.4% of listings a year earlier to 43.5%, with Denver at 71.9%. If your building competes with new supply, you'll probably match those offers.

Run the math. One free month on a 12-month lease cuts what the tenant actually pays by about 8.3%. A building can hit 90% occupancy and still miss its income target.

Plan for this:

- **Assume the takeout lender looks at collected rent,** not asking rent.
- **Front-load concessions** on early leases, so they burn off before the seasoning window.
- **Watch renewals.** A tenant who got a free month will push back on a full-price renewal.

## Renovate in phases. Keep the income.

Don't empty the building. Vacant units bring in nothing while the interest keeps accruing.

Turn units in batches, 3 or 4 at a time for a building this size. Finish a batch, photograph it, list it, then start the next one. The occupied units keep paying interest and operating costs while the work goes on.

Draws set the pace. If the contractor waits on reimbursement, the next batch waits too. Line up your [rehab draw schedule](/blog/rehab-draw-schedule) with the batch plan before closing. Keep holding costs in view while you do it.

## The refinance clock in a tighter market

The permanent loan market is tougher than when many bridge loans were written. HousingWire reported on August 18, 2026 that lenders now underwrite with higher rates, lower leverage and stronger debt-service coverage. In the same report, owners who fall short were left choosing between new equity, an extension or a discounted sale.

Coverage is the second test after occupancy. Fannie Mae's Conventional Properties term sheet (2026) lists a minimum DSCR of 1.25x. Run your stabilized net operating income against that before you buy. Our guide on [how to calculate DSCR](/blog/how-to-calculate-dscr) shows the formula.

Set decision points on the calendar:

- **Month 6:** Are renovations on budget? If not, re-forecast the lease-up start.
- **Month 9:** Is occupancy on track for the seasoning window? If not, call your lender about extension terms now.
- **90 days before maturity:** Takeout application in, appraisal ordered.

If maturity is close and you're short, read [what happens at extension, maturity and default](/blog/hard-money-loan-extension-maturity-default) before you need it.
