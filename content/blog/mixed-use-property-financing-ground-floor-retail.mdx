Ground-floor retail is either the best income in the building or the reason the appraisal comes in short. Same storefront. Different lease file.

Miami gives you a strong starting point. According to MIAMI REALTORS, in a report published October 7, 2026, the Miami market area had a 3.0% retail vacancy rate in Q2 2026, half the 6.0% national rate, based on Cushman & Wakefield data. But a metro average won't lease your storefront. Below: how lenders value the commercial piece, where it adds to your loan, where it drags, and how to package the deal so the storefront works for you.

## The Short Answer

Retail helps when it's leased, the rent is documented, and it's a minority of the building's income.

Retail hurts when it's vacant, when it carries most of the income, or when the space only works for one kind of tenant.

Lenders size loans on income they can prove. An empty storefront proves nothing.

## Why Lenders Value the Storefront Differently

Lenders capitalize apartment income and retail income at different cap rates, and the retail dollar usually comes out worth less. Matthews' South Florida retail report for Q2 2026, published in July 2026, put the regional retail cap rate at 6.5%. Miami Finance Review, published in July 2026, put South Florida multifamily cap rates near 5.0% based on Q1 data.

Here's what that gap does to a building. Take eight apartments over a 2,000-square-foot storefront. The numbers are illustrative, not a quote.

| Illustrative | Residential | Retail |
|---|---|---|
| Net operating income | $120,000 | $60,000 |
| Cap rate | 5.0% | 6.5% |
| Implied value | $2,400,000 | $923,077 |

Total value: $3,323,077. If the $60,000 of retail income were capped at the apartment rate, it would support $1,200,000. Same rent, $276,923 less value, because it comes from a storefront. If cap rates are new to you, start with [how to calculate cap rate](/blog/how-to-calculate-cap-rate).

Now look at coverage. Say annual debt service is $130,000 (also illustrative).

- Retail leased: $180,000 NOI / $130,000 = **1.38 DSCR**
- Retail vacant: $120,000 NOI / $130,000 = **0.92 DSCR**

The building is the same in both cases. The only difference is whether one tenant is paying rent. That's why a lender reads the commercial leases before anything else. For the math, see [how to calculate DSCR](/blog/how-to-calculate-dscr).

## Miami's Retail Numbers Help. Read the Submarket Anyway.

The county-level data is about as good as it gets. Commercial Observer reported on July 16, 2026, citing Colliers, that Miami-Dade retail vacancy fell to 3% in Q2 2026, with average asking rents up 2.6% from the prior quarter to $42.50 per square foot per year.

The averages hide some soft spots. Marcus & Millichap's 1Q 2026 Miami retail report said the city of Miami, downtown included, saw vacancy rise by more than 100 basis points in 2025 and started 2026 above 5%. New supply is coming too. MIAMI REALTORS counted 1.1 million square feet of South Florida retail under construction as of September 2026.

The appraiser won't use the county average. They'll use leases and sales on your block and in your corridor. A storefront in a busy neighborhood strip and one on a stretch of new towers with empty shell space can look alike on paper. Lenders will value them very differently.

## Where Ground-Floor Retail Helps

**It's leased and seasoned.** The tenant is in place, paying rent, and the rent shows up in the trailing 12 months. Lenders trust bank deposits more than a signed lease with no history. Here's [what lenders read in a rent roll and T12](/blog/rent-roll-t12-what-lenders-read).

**It's a minority of income.** When the apartments carry the debt service alone, the storefront is upside. A lender can stress the retail to zero and the deal still covers.

**The tenant serves the residents.** A laundromat, a coffee shop, a pharmacy counter. Daily-needs tenants fit a residential building and are easier to replace.

**The space is generic.** A plain shell with storefront glass can go to many tenants. That widens the pool if the current one leaves.

**The corridor has proven foot traffic.** Comparable leases nearby give the appraiser data to support your rent.

## Where It Hurts

**It's vacant at closing.** Spec retail means no income to underwrite. Expect the lender to count little or nothing for it until a tenant signs and pays. A value-add lease-up is usually a bridge conversation, not a long-term one. See [multifamily bridge loans for value-add](/blog/multifamily-bridge-loan-value-add).

**Retail carries the deal.** If the building only covers its debt with the storefront rent, one move-out puts the loan underwater on coverage. The bigger the commercial share, the more the building gets underwritten like a commercial property.

**The build-out is specialized.** Restaurant hoods, grease traps and medical plumbing cost a lot to install and limit who can reuse the space. Lenders and appraisers know a second-generation restaurant space doesn't fit most tenants.

**Zoning made you build it.** In Miami's urban zones, the storefront often isn't optional. A Miami 21 developer guide from Acreus, published in 2026, notes that ground-floor retail or liner space is required along designated frontages in T5 and T6 zones. The adopted Miami 21 Article 5 standards require retail frontage facades to be detailed as storefronts with at least 70% clear glass at sidewalk level. Required space still has to find a tenant, and your pro forma has to carry it while it sits empty.

**Insurance gets harder.** Commercial occupancy changes the policy, and flood coverage can fall short. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide topic B4-2.1-03, dated August 5, 2026, warns that condo projects in Special Flood Hazard Areas with more than 25% commercial space may need supplemental or private flood coverage, because NFIP coverage may not be enough. Get the quote before you go hard on the contract. Our [investment property insurance requirements](/blog/investment-property-insurance-requirements) post covers the basics.

## The Rules That Cap the Commercial Share

Two rules matter most if you build or exit in Florida.

**Live Local Act.** A Holland & Knight analysis published on Lexology in July 2025 explains that since July 1, 2023, the Act has required local governments to allow multifamily and mixed-use projects on commercial, industrial or mixed-use sites when at least 40% of units are affordable to households earning up to 120% of area median income for at least 30 years. The same analysis notes that the 2025 amendments bar local governments from requiring more than 10% of a mixed-use project's total square footage to be nonresidential. For a Live Local project, that rule lets you keep the storefront small and put the square footage into units.

**Agency limits shape your exit buyers.** Fannie Mae's Selling Guide topic B4-2.1-03, dated August 5, 2026, caps commercial or mixed-use space at 35% of a condo or co-op project or of the building it sits in. If you plan to condo out the residential floors, an oversized commercial component can shrink your buyer pool. If you build ground-up, run the [residual land value](/blog/residual-land-value-price-the-lot-first) math with the commercial share fixed first.

## How to Package a Mixed-Use Deal for a Lender

1. **Send every commercial lease in full.** Start date, term, renewal options, rent bumps, and who pays taxes, insurance and common-area costs.
2. **Split the T12.** Residential income in one column and commercial in another. Lenders will separate them anyway, so show you've done it.
3. **Prove the use is legal.** Zoning verification and the certificate of use for the commercial space.
4. **Price insurance early.** Commercial occupancy, liability and flood, quoted on the actual building.
5. **Show the downside.** What the building covers if the storefront goes dark for six months. If the answer is "it still covers," say so on page one.
