Every flip spreadsheet has two big numbers in it: what you paid and what the rehab costs. Both get scrutinized. Both get bid out.

Then there is the third number, the one that shows up in month seven and is almost never in the model at the time of the offer. Carry.

Holding costs do not ruin deals dramatically. They erode them quietly, at a few thousand dollars a month, until a 25% gross margin turns into something you would not have bought. ATTOM's Q1 2026 U.S. Home Flipping Report, published June 18, 2026, put gross profit per flip at **$66,000** — down from **$74,172** a year earlier. The cushion for schedule slippage is thinner than it was. This post builds the number properly.

## The Eleven Line Items

Most investors budget three of these. Here is the full list.

| # | Line item | Typical basis | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Loan interest | Monthly on drawn balance | Usually the largest single line |
| 2 | Property taxes | Annual ÷ 12 | Often reassessed after sale |
| 3 | Insurance | Annual premium ÷ 12 | Builder's risk, not a vacant policy |
| 4 | Utilities | Monthly | Power and water must stay on for trades |
| 5 | HOA or condo dues | Monthly | Plus any special assessment in force |
| 6 | Lawn and pool | Monthly | Code enforcement cares in Florida |
| 7 | Security or monitoring | Monthly | Cameras, lockboxes, board-up |
| 8 | Permit and inspection fees | Per project | Re-inspection fees stack up |
| 9 | Staging | Monthly during listing | Rental furniture is a carry line |
| 10 | Listing and marketing | Per listing | Photos, drone, floor plan |
| 11 | Contingency | 10% of the above | For the month you did not plan on |

The contingency line at the bottom is not padding. It is the arithmetic acknowledgment that the permit gets kicked back, the inspector reschedules, or the appraiser is booked out three weeks — none of which is anyone's fault and all of which costs a month.

Two of these deserve a flag. Insurance belongs in the carry budget at the builder's risk premium, not at a vacant dwelling rate — the two policies cover different things during active renovation, and getting it wrong is both a coverage gap and a budget error. We broke that down in [builder's risk vs. vacant dwelling](/blog/builders-risk-vs-vacant-dwelling). And utilities are not optional: no power means no trades on site, which means the schedule slips, which raises every other line on this table.

## Build the Monthly Burn Before You Bid

Total the eleven lines, annualize what needs annualizing, and divide by twelve. That single number is your burn rate, and it is the most useful figure in your model because it converts time directly into dollars.

**Illustrative example.** A $250K loan at 10%, $4,200 in annual property taxes, $2,800 for builder's risk, $180 a month in utilities, and $120 a month for lawn and security:

| Line | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Interest ($250K at 10%) | $2,083 |
| Property taxes ($4,200 ÷ 12) | $350 |
| Insurance ($2,800 ÷ 12) | $233 |
| Utilities, lawn, security | $300 |
| **Monthly burn** | **$2,966** |

Call it roughly **$3,000 a month**. Now the only question that matters is how many months you are buying.

## What Every Extra 30 Days Costs

Here is the same deal at four different hold lengths.

| Hold | Months | Total carry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 120 days | 4.0 | $11,864 |
| 150 days | 5.0 | $14,830 |
| 165 days (national average) | 5.5 | $16,313 |
| 195 days | 6.5 | $19,279 |

The gap between the 120-day plan and the 165-day reality is **$4,449**. Nobody made a mistake. No contractor walked off. The calendar did that, on a deal where the entire gross profit might be $40K.

And 165 is not a pessimistic number — it is the current national average days to flip in ATTOM's Q1 2026 report, up from 160 days in Q4 2025. The resale side is not getting faster either: Realtor.com's August 2026 housing report, published September 2, 2026, put national median days on market at **60 days**, with **20.4%** of listings taking a price cut. Your rehab timeline is only half of the hold.

> **Pro tip:** Build your holding budget on 165 days, not 120. Pricing your carry to the optimistic number is how a 25% gross margin becomes a 12% net one — and ATTOM's 25.4% Q1 2026 return is a *gross* figure, before rehab and carrying costs. The net is what you keep.

## Where Holding Costs Hide Inside the 70% Rule

The [70% rule](/blog/70-percent-rule-house-flipping) says your maximum offer is 70% of ARV minus rehab. The 30% haircut is doing a lot of work: it is meant to cover holding costs, selling costs, financing costs, and your profit, all in one move.

On a $340K ARV with $60K of rehab, the rule gives you a $178K maximum offer and a $102K gross spread. Take out roughly $16K of carry at 165 days, about $24K in selling costs at 7%, and your financing points, and the profit left is real but a lot smaller than $102K looked.

That is fine — the rule is a screen, not a model. The failure mode is treating the 30% as pure profit and then discovering the carry inside it in month five.

Two adjustments make the screen honest. First, in a high-tax or high-insurance market, the 30% haircut is too thin on any hold past four months; underwrite the carry as its own line and let the rule tell you only whether the deal is worth modeling at all. Second, carry scales with the loan balance, not with the purchase price — so a deal financed at up to 90% LTC carries more per month than the same deal at 75%, even though the rule produces the identical maximum offer. Higher leverage buys you deal count and costs you burn rate. That is a real trade, and it should be a decision rather than a default. Build the [rehab estimate](/blog/how-to-estimate-rehab-costs) and the carry line separately, and run both sides of the [closing costs](/blog/hard-money-loan-closing-costs) too. A first screen and a real underwrite are different documents.

## Five Ways to Cut the Burn

1. **Time your draws.** Interest accrues on funds you have drawn. Requesting the full rehab up front and letting it sit is paying for money that is not working yet. Match the [draw schedule](/blog/rehab-draw-schedule) to the actual construction sequence.
2. **Right-size utilities.** Keep power and water on, but drop to the smallest service tier the trades can work on. Skip cable, pool heat, and irrigation you do not need.
3. **List before the punch list is done.** Photos and marketing can start while final trim and touch-up are in progress. Two weeks of overlap is two weeks of carry you never pay.
4. **Appeal the assessment.** If the county set value on the pre-rehab condition and then reassessed mid-project, it is worth a call. Taxes are one of the few carry lines with a paper appeal.
5. **Ask for an interest reserve or a funded first draw.** Both move cash requirements off your balance sheet in the early months. Neither is free, but on a long hold the arithmetic often favors them.
